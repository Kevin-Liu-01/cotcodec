# Angle B — portability factorization after PorTAL (inventor: B-portability-after-portal, 2026-09-01)

Angle: the next factorization after PorTAL — port across ARCHITECTURE FAMILIES (softmax transformer ↔ GDN/KDA/DeltaNet/Mamba ↔ masked diffusion ↔ byte model), across tokenizers, or port update RULES/states rather than weights. Synthesis gaps targeted: G3, G4, G5 (plus G2/G7 as side products of candidate B).

Honesty conventions. "No direct prior art found through 2026-09-01 under <coverage>" is the only novelty wording used. Every prior below was opened at its primary page during this session (WebFetch on arxiv.org/abs or the Ramp blog); numbers are first-party unless a venue is stated. Search coverage and its limits are in §5.

## 0. What the three candidates factor, and why they are not the occupied things

PorTAL's move is adapter = core(task latent) ∘ per-base alignment, fitted with labeled task data, among softmax-attention transformers (Qwen3 → Qwen3-8B / Gemma-3 / Gemma-4-E2B / Mistral / Inkling). Three axes of "what varies between the source and the target" were left unmeasured:

| Axis varied | Candidate | What is held fixed | Why this is not the occupied "train once, port a LoRA" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sequence operator (softmax attention → GDN 3:1 hybrid → single-attention GDN → GLA / DeltaNet / TTT fast weights / Mamba) | A `task-latent-operator-port` | Tokenizer (byte-identical Llama-2 32k vocabulary across the whole fla-hub / startlux / E²-TTT ladder), width (1024 or 2048), depth (24) | PorTAL/Cross-LoRA/Trans-LoRA/LoRA-X/TiTok all stay inside softmax transformers; base-conversion papers (Attention-to-Mamba, HyLo, Priming, Taylor-Calibrate, HALO) move base weights, never fine-tunes; UpgradeBench states "shape-incompatible hops admit no weight-space method" and measures none. |
| Update dynamics (HOW a model updates in context) | B `icl-rule-distillation-port` (moonshot) | Bases frozen; same interface on every base; same demonstrations | D16 meta-trains a rule on prequential tool-outcome loss; nothing distills a pretrained transformer's implicit in-context update into an explicit rule and ports it (learned-update-rules G6; adapter G4). Engram reader transfer / MentorPulse / KV translation port state or a frozen artifact, not a rule. |
| Segmentation (subword vocabularies of different fertility → bytes) | C `byte-span-anchored-adapter-port` | Operator family (from-scratch triplet with identical architecture and data; frozen transformer pairs) | PorTAL crosses tokenizers silently ("each base uses its own tokenizer") and never analyzes it; cross-tokenizer work lives at the logit/distillation level (ALM, BPM, BLD, ACTD) or vocabulary adaptation (TokAlign++, Franken-Adapter), not at the adapter-alignment level. |

D16 (Portable Sidecar Update Dynamics) is STILL_OPEN (narrow) per killshot-current; candidate B sharpens it rather than duplicating it: the rule is obtained by distilling a specific transformer's in-context behaviour (a portable HOW with a named source), the identification signal is translation-equivariance of writes, and the sealed cell is "same demonstrations, no context, other operator family". D16's prequential-loss rule becomes a mandatory sibling control inside B.

## 2. Candidate A — `task-latent-operator-port` (cheap-decisive; claim_scope: portability-protocol)

**Claim.** A PorTAL-class task latent trained on softmax transformers ports onto linear-attention, hybrid, TTT and SSM bases through a label-free functional alignment at attention-pathway q/v sites, and the retained fraction of fresh-LoRA lift is a monotone function of the fraction of the target's sequence-mixing layers that are softmax attention.

**Mechanism.** Source training follows PorTAL on two softmax sources S1 = fla-hub/transformer-340M-10B and S2 = fla-hub/transformer-1.3B-100B: task latent z_τ ∈ R^256; shared core G emits rank-8 LoRA factors for q_proj and v_proj at every source layer, ΔW^S(ℓ) = B^S(ℓ) A^S(ℓ) with A^S ∈ R^{8×d_S}, B^S ∈ R^{d_out×8}, trained with task labels exactly as PorTAL (12 balanced portallib tasks after dropping/shuffling TruthfulQA and SciQ, issue #28). The target-side alignment is label-free. For a target T and a site ℓ_T with matched depth fraction ℓ_T/L_T ≈ ℓ_S/L_S, run N = 2,000 unlabeled calibration sequences x through both frozen bases and record residual inputs h_S(x,t), h_T(x,t) at every token position t (same tokenizer, so positions coincide on the iso-tokenizer ladder). Fit the read map R_in = argmin_R Σ_{x,t} ||R h_T(x,t) − h_S(x,t)||² + λ||R||² (ridge, or whitened orthogonal Procrustes with the signed-permutation gauge check of 2606.31963), and transport the input factor A^T = A^S R_in, so that A^T h_T ≈ A^S h_S. The output factor is obtained by Theseus-style functional matching: the source adapter's residual effect δ_S(x,t) = f_ℓ^S(x; W+ΔW) − f_ℓ^S(x; W) is mapped into target coordinates by R_out = argmin_R Σ ||R o_S − o_T||² (fitted on the same unlabeled set), and B^T = argmin_B Σ_{x,t} || f_ℓ^T(x; W_T + B A^T) − f_ℓ^T(x; W_T) − R_out δ_S(x,t) ||² solved by ≤300 gradient steps on the calibration set only. Sites: (i) attention-pathway only — the softmax layers of hybrids (8 of 24 in the 3:1 GDN hybrid; layer 12 only in the single-attention GDN; all 24 in the transformer control); (ii) for GLA / DeltaNet / E²-TTT, the operator's own q_proj/v_proj; (iii) for Mamba, in_proj (x branch) as the v analogue and out_proj as the output site. Retention r = (acc_ported − acc_base)/(acc_freshLoRA − acc_base), macro over tasks, per target; f_attn = softmax layers / total mixing layers ∈ {1, 1/3, 1/24, 0}. Second substrate (multilingual, cross-tokenizer): Qwen3-0.6B-Base → Qwen3.5-4B (24 GDN + 8 full attention), where the calibration set is parallel sentences and the alignment is fitted on meaning-class means μ_b(m) = (1/K) Σ_k h̄_b(x_{m,k}) over K = 4 language versions of sentence m, compared with equal-token monolingual English calibration.

**What is new (delta vs. three closest priors).** PorTAL fits the alignment with labeled task data (up to 2,000 examples per task) and only onto softmax-attention bases; here the alignment is label-free and the targets are non-softmax operators, with f_attn as a dose–response variable. Theseus transports a single static task vector across widths within one family by functional matching; here the transported object is a hypernetwork-emitted latent (one core, many tasks) and the transport crosses operator families with an explicit site map onto delta-rule/SSM projections. "Where Should LoRA Go?" adapts hybrids natively and finds the attention pathway is the safe site; here that finding is used as a prior and tested for adapters that never saw the hybrid. No direct prior art found through 2026-09-01 under §5 coverage for porting a task adapter onto a KDA/GDN/GLA/DeltaNet/TTT/Mamba base.

**Closest priors (all opened).**
1. PorTAL — https://labs.ramp.com/research/portal-portable-task-adaptation/ — 2026-07-01 (no arXiv paper as of 2026-09-01; artifacts one seed; issue #28 gold-index defect) — labeled refit, softmax bases only.
2. Theseus: Transporting Task Vectors across Different Architectures without Training — https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12952 — v1 2026-02-13, ICML 2026 — label-free functional transport across widths within a family; T5-3B→T5-Large 57.87→76.78 (FT 87.92).
3. Where Should LoRA Go? — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22127 — 2026-04-24 — attention-pathway LoRA beats full adaptation with 5–10× fewer parameters; recurrent-pathway adaptation −14.8 pp GSM8K in the sequential hybrid (Qwen3.5-0.8B), +8.6 pp in the parallel hybrid (Falcon-H1-0.5B); nothing transferred from a transformer.
4. Cross-Model Memory Transfer via Target-Side Reader Adaptation — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17050 — 2026-08-17 — frozen memory artifact + thin labeled reader across transformer backbones (Mistral-7B: cross-model 38.5 = same-model 38.5); ports state, not adapters, and not to non-transformers. Mandatory control.

**Falsifiable predictions.**
- P1 (dose–response). On the 340M iso-tokenizer ladder, retention is monotone in f_attn: transformer-340M control ≥ 0.85; GDN 3:1 hybrid (attention sites only) ≥ 0.50; single-attention GDN between 0.20 and 0.50; GLA, E²-TTT ≤ 0.30; Spearman ρ(f_attn, retention) ≥ 0.8 over ≥ 6 targets × 12 tasks.
- P2 (site prior). Adding the GDN layers' q/v sites to the hybrid port lowers retention by ≥ 0.10 relative to attention-only sites.
- P3 (label-free cost). Label-free functional alignment reaches ≥ 70% of what PorTAL's label-fitted alignment (1,000 labeled examples/task) reaches on the same target, and exceeds Cross-LoRA (PorTAL reports ~14% recovered lift) and Theseus single-task transport by ≥ 0.20 retention.
- P4 (multilingual pair). Meaning-class calibration on 4-language parallel sentences beats equal-token monolingual English calibration by ≥ 0.05 retention on non-English task translations and by ≤ 0.02 on English.

**Kill conditions.** Hybrid attention-site retention ≤ 0.20 (no better than Cross-LoRA/Theseus); ρ(f_attn, retention) < 0.3 (operator family does not govern portability, so the mechanism claim is wrong even if some porting works); random-latent (alignment-only) or latent-swap controls recover ≥ 50% of the ported lift; the gain disappears on held-out prompt formats (2608.09490); fresh LoRA trained on 64 labeled target examples matches or beats the label-free port.

**Cheapest decisive pilot.** Phase 0 (CPU, no LM): (a) transport algebra on a random 2-layer transformer/DeltaNet pair — A^T h_T = A^S h_S under a planted rotation, invariance under signed permutations, closed-form B^T recovery; (b) tokenizer doctors — vocabulary diffs already show 32,000 identical (piece,id) pairs across the ladder and 50,254 for Pythia/Mamba; (c) task-suite audit — shuffle choices per row, verify a per-index prior scores at chance (issue #28), fix `rows[:1000]` slicing (issue #27); (d) gradient-access audit that alignment fits never read labels. Phase 1 (GPU, ≤ 15 H100-h): core training on S1+S2 (~3 h); fresh per-task LoRA oracles 12 tasks × 7 targets (~4 h); label-free alignments for 7 targets (~2.5 h); Cross-LoRA and Theseus baselines (~1 h); PorTAL label-fitted alignment reference on two targets (~1.5 h); Qwen3-0.6B-Base → Qwen3.5-4B meaning-class vs monolingual alignment (~3 h). Three seeds on alignments and evaluations; the core is trained once (pre-registered) — a stated limit. Registry ids: qwen3-0.6b-base, qwen3.5-4b, mamba-130m-hf, delta-net-1.3b-8k, plus the pins in §1.

**Controls.** Fresh per-task LoRA (labels; oracle); Cross-LoRA (2508.05232); Theseus (2602.12952); PorTAL label-fitted alignment (upper reference); Engram-style frozen source adapter + thin labeled reader (2608.17050); KV-translation-style activation port (2608.30963: port the adapted source's residual activations, not weights); Hyper-X-style target-native language×task adapter (labeled) on the multilingual pair; random-latent / latent-swap / alignment-only; held-out prompt formats (2608.09490); two-forward-pass prefix-invariance audit on adapted hybrids (2608.22876); iso-parameter (alignment params vs LoRA params) and iso-wall-time ledgers; per-arm HP search at the 340M rung; 3 seeds with paired clustered SEs.

**Kevin advantage.** Honest split: on the iso-tokenizer English ladder, none beyond the 8×H100 node and the sealed task×base harness (any lab could run it). Parallel corpora matter only on the multilingual Qwen pair (meaning-class calibration, translation-paired evaluation). Tinker is a possible source of larger softmax-side adapters (Qwen3.5 bases are hybrids, so Tinker LoRAs on them would serve the reverse hybrid→transformer port) if checkpoint export is used; no Tinker access to hybrid internals is needed.

**Collision risk: medium.** Searches run (host relay, 2026-09-01): arXiv `(LoRA|adapter) AND (Mamba|"linear attention"|DeltaNet|"state space"|hybrid) AND (transfer|port|transplant|transport) AND transformer AND ("fine-tuned"|"task vector"|"task vectors")` → 25 results, only 2604.22127 (native placement) and conversion papers (2510.05901, 2606.16429, 2601.22156) relevant; HF papers "transfer LoRA adapter from transformer to Mamba state space hybrid linear attention model" → same plus MambaVision/TransMamba (no adapter transfer); DuckDuckGo query returned no results (likely blocked). Merging groups with live cross-family alignment code (HeteroFusion pushed 2026-09-01; Transport-and-Merge) could extend to adapters within months.

**Monitorability and safety.** Weight-space adapters; no change to the reasoning medium. Ported adapters may carry source-side behaviours across bases, so a refusal/harm regression on each target before and after porting is mandatory. Data rights: portallib-tasks are public; fla-hub transformer-340M and delta_net carry no stated license (publication requires resolution; startlux Apache-2.0, E²-TTT MIT, Bolmo Apache-2.0); parallel corpora are used only as unlabeled calibration stimulus and are not redistributed.

**Negative-result value.** If f_attn does not govern portability, either task skills live in operator-agnostic residual directions (any base is a target; UpgradeBench's "no weight-space method" claim is refuted for learned mappings) or porting fails everywhere (the claim is confirmed empirically for learned mappings too). Either way the first sealed multi-family task×base portability cell exists (adapter-portability G5), with shuffled-choice hygiene the field's only benchmark lacks.

## 3. Candidate B — `icl-rule-distillation-port` (moonshot; claim_scope: architecture-causal)

**Claim.** A pretrained transformer's implicit in-context update can be distilled into an explicit canonical fast-weight rule — a base-independent 64-dimensional associative state plus a learned write rule — that, frozen, ports through label-free read/write maps onto bases of other operator families and reproduces a measurable fraction of their in-context gains with no context in the window; parallel translations identify that the rule writes content rather than surface form.

**Mechanism.** Let T be a frozen transformer and c_1..c_n a demonstration stream of formatted (x_i, y_i) pairs with probe queries q ∈ Q. The transformer's implicit update at step i is the change in its function, Δ_i(q) = log p_T(·|q, c_{≤i}) − log p_T(·|q, c_{<i}). The canonical memory is M^k ∈ R^{64×64}, rank-8 factorized, at K = 4 residual sites k (depth fractions 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0); base b attaches through task-blind rank-8 maps P_b^k ∈ R^{64×d_b} (read) and Q_b^k ∈ R^{d_b×64} (write). Read at every query position: h^k ← h^k + Q_b^k M^k P_b^k h^k. Write, once per demonstration and strictly after the probe of step i−1: with canonical key k_i = P_b^k h̄^k(x_i) (mean over the x_i tokens), value v_i = P_b^k h̄^k(y_i) and error e_i = v_i − M^k k_i, the shared rule R_θ emits (ρ_i, η_i, u_i, w_i) = R_θ(k_i, v_i, e_i, M^k k_i, i) and M^k_{i+1} = Π_8[ρ_i M^k_i + η_i u_i w_iᵀ], Π_8 = rank-8 truncation. Derived-rule baselines occupy the same interface: delta rule (u = e, w = k, fixed η, ρ = 1), Kaczmarz step η_i = η/(||k_i||² + ε), OSDN diagonal preconditioning, Falcon-1/2/3 normalized rules. Distillation (the new step): with the source base being T itself and the demonstrations removed from the window, minimize L_dist = Σ_i Σ_q KL( p_T(·|q, c_{≤i}) || p_{T,M_i}(·|q) ) + λ_cause·L_cause over θ, P_T, Q_T with truncated BPTT through 8 writes, where L_cause penalizes any read of M_{i} by probes issued before write i (startup-causality gate from the SR-TTT retraction). Translation-equivariance (parallel data): for a demonstration c_i and its translation c_i^(B), L_eq = ||ΔM(c_i) − ΔM(c_i^(B))||_F²; total loss L = L_dist + λ_eq L_eq; the evaluation "write-A / read-B" presents demonstrations in language A and probes in language B. Port: freeze R_θ; for a target b' of another family fit only P_{b'}, Q_{b'} label-free by the functional alignment of candidate A (P_{b'} = P_T R_in, Q_{b'} fitted by output matching) on unlabeled text that contains no task family used in evaluation; evaluate b' with no context after i writes against (i) b' native in-context with the same demonstrations, (ii) derived rules with the same interface and state bytes, (iii) a D16-style rule meta-trained on prequential loss (sibling), (iv) MentorPulse-style live mentor (T present at inference; upper reference). Effective step size s_i = η_i ||u_i|| ||w_i|| is logged per write.

**What is new (delta vs. closest priors).** "Can Gradient Descent Simulate Prompting?" meta-trains the same LM so that a gradient step on its own weights mimics conditioning; here the update is externalized into a canonical rule with a base-independent interface and ported frozen to other operator families. "Learning without training" derives analytically that context equals a low-rank MLP update inside one transformer block; here the update is learned as a rule by behavioural distillation and tested cross-family. Algorithm Distillation (and its Mamba scaling) distills a learning algorithm's histories INTO a sequence model's in-context behaviour; here the direction is reversed — OUT of a transformer's in-context behaviour into an explicit recurrent write rule. Attention-to-Mamba / Taylor-Calibrate / HALO distill base weights (WHAT); here bases stay frozen and only HOW is moved. Modular TTT factorizes the inner rule but never transfers it; D16 meta-trains a rule on outcome loss without a named source algorithm. No direct prior art found through 2026-09-01 under §5 coverage for distilling a transformer's in-context update into a portable explicit rule.

**Closest priors (all opened).**
1. Can Gradient Descent Simulate Prompting? — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20989 — 2025-06-26 — same-model gradient step emulates conditioning; recovers "some (and occasionally all)" of prompted performance.
2. Learning without training: The implicit dynamics of in-context learning — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16003 — v1 2025-07-21, v4 2026-06-02 — forward pass with context ≡ forward pass without context plus a minimal low-rank MLP update (analytic, single model).
3. In-context RL with Algorithm Distillation — https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14215 — 2022-10-25 — and Scaling Algorithm Distillation for Continuous Control with Mamba — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13892 — 2025-06-16 — algorithm → sequence model (in-context), Mamba as the AD student; no explicit fast-weight rule.
4. Attention to Mamba: A Recipe for Cross-Architecture Distillation — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14191 — 2026-04-01 — Pythia-1B → Mamba base distillation (ppl 14.11 vs 13.86); ports WHAT.
5. Modular TTT — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07110 — 2026-08-07 — inner learner as a DAG; 410M/1.45B at 100B tokens comparable to GDN; no cross-base transfer (control substrate). Also MentorPulse — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20927 — 2026-08-21 — mentor must be present at inference; transformers only (upper-reference control).

**Falsifiable predictions.**
- P1 (identifiability on the source). On sealed held-out in-context task families (8 demonstrations), the distilled rule with no context reproduces ≥ 70% of the transformer's few-shot-over-zero-shot gain, whereas the best derived rule (delta / Kaczmarz / OSDN / Falcon) at the same interface and state bytes reproduces ≤ 40%.
- P2 (port ordering). On the iso-tokenizer 340M targets with label-free P/Q, the ported rule recovers ≥ 0.50 of the GDN 3:1 hybrid's own native in-context gain, 0.30–0.50 on GLA/E²-TTT/DeltaNet, ≤ 0.30 on Mamba, and beats the best derived rule by ≥ 0.10 on the hybrid and DeltaNet (per-family ICL theory: GD-emulation vs online-GD/Bayes-filter mismatch).
- P3 (equivariance). With λ_eq > 0, write-A/read-B accuracy on Qwen3-0.6B-Base → Qwen3.5-4B is within 5 points of write-A/read-A; with λ_eq = 0 the gap is ≥ 15 points.
- P4 (dynamics signature). The logged effective step size follows s_i ∝ i^{−γ} with γ = 1.0 ± 0.3 (diminishing marginal in-context gain), which no fixed-η derived rule reproduces.

**Kill conditions.** P1 fails (derived rule ≈ distilled rule): the transformer's in-context HOW at this interface is indistinguishable from GD/delta, and nothing beyond hand-derived rules is portable — publish as a behavioural ICL-as-GD confirmation and stop; ported rule ≤ best derived rule on every target; the startup-causality or two-forward-pass prefix-invariance audit (2608.22876) finds leakage; a single adversarial demonstration persists in M across the declared reset (poisoning/reset red line, G7); L_eq > 0 costs > 10 points of monolingual accuracy.

**Cheapest decisive pilot.** Phase 0 (CPU, fp64): synthetic in-context linear regression (d = 8): train a 2-layer softmax transformer on ICL regression; distill its implicit update into R_θ with a 16-d canonical state; compare its error curve with one-step GD and RLS; attach the frozen rule to a tiny DeltaNet trained on the same task distribution; causality self-test (probes cannot see their own step's write). Phase 1 (≤ 4 H100-h): pythia-160m → mamba-130m-hf (identical GPT-NeoX vocabulary; both Pile-trained) — distill on Pythia, port to Mamba, derived-rule and native-ICL references. Phase 2 (≤ 8 H100-h): fla-hub/transformer-340M-10B → gdn-340m-isp-hybrid-3to1-10b, gla-340M-15B, e2-ttt-swiglu-340M-15B; D16-style prequential sibling and MentorPulse-style upper reference. Phase 3 (≤ 4 H100-h): equivariance and write-A/read-B on Qwen3-0.6B-Base → Qwen3.5-4B with parallel demonstrations. Total ≤ 16 H100-h; 3 seeds on distillation and ports; SIGUSR1-resumable truncated BPTT.

## 4. Candidate C — `byte-span-anchored-adapter-port` (cheap-decisive; claim_scope: portability-protocol)

**Claim.** Adapter portability across tokenizers is governed by what the alignment is anchored on: anchoring the label-free alignment on byte spans linked across languages by parallel span alignments makes ported-adapter lift retention invariant to tokenizer fertility, whereas token-position anchoring degrades linearly in |log fertility ratio|; the effect is identifiable on a from-scratch matched-tokenizer triplet and extends to a byte-level model.

**Mechanism.** Anchor units are byte spans a ⊂ bytes(x) (words or phrases) with cross-lingual links ℓ(a) from span alignments over parallel sentences (OmniAlign 2608.18474 / CTFAlign 2608.21023); an anchor class is {a, ℓ(a)} — the same meaning in two languages. For any base b with segmentation seg_b (subword tokens, byte patches, or raw bytes) the anchor representation at site ℓ is r_b^ℓ(a) = (1/|I_b(a)|) Σ_{t ∈ I_b(a)} h_b^ℓ(t), I_b(a) = {t : bytes(t) ∩ a ≠ ∅} — byte-range intersection pooling, defined for every segmentation. Alignment: R_T^ℓ = argmin_R Σ_a ||R r_T^ℓ(a) − r_S^ℓ(a)||² + λ||R||² over anchor classes (with the meaning-class option, both language members share one class mean). Token-position anchoring is the baseline: R fitted on position-matched token pairs (identical tokenizers) or on greedy byte-prefix-aligned token pairs (different tokenizers, as in cross-tokenizer distillation). The source task adapter (a PorTAL-emitted or plain LoRA) is transported as in candidate A: A^T = A^S R_T at the matched depth; B^T by functional matching of anchor-pooled deltas, B^T = argmin_B Σ_a ||δ_T(a; B A^T) − R_out δ_S(a)||². Fertility distance D(S,T; L) = |log(fert_T(L)/fert_S(L))|, fert = tokens per byte on language L. Hypothesis H_C: retention_token(L) = r_0 − κ·D(S,T;L) with κ > 0, while retention_byte(L) = r_0' with κ' ≈ 0. Identification: three 60M models trained from scratch on identical multilingual bytes (10 languages including Hindi, Thai, Tamil; parallel corpora plus a FineWeb-2 slice; equal bytes seen), differing only in segmentation — raw bytes, BPE-8k, BPE-32k, both BPEs with the `\p{L}+` pre-tokenizer fixed (2608.26449); adapters trained on the BPE-32k model are ported to the other two under both anchorings. Frozen extension: Qwen3-0.6B-Base (151,936) → Bolmo-1B (bytes; port at the 16 latent full-attention layers' q_proj/v_proj) and → Qwen3.5-4B (248,320; also crosses operator — decomposed jointly with candidate A's iso-tokenizer operator cost); BLT-1B for discovery only.

**What is new (delta vs. closest priors).** Transport-and-Merge aligns activations by optimal transport on neuron correspondences to fuse base weights across families and tokenizers; here the anchors are byte spans linked by parallel alignments, the object moved is a task adapter, and fertility-dependence is the measured law. Byte-Level Distillation and ACTD transfer knowledge across tokenizers by distilling output distributions through a byte interface or anchor loss with the teacher running at transfer time; here adapter weights move through a representation-level alignment without the source model at transfer time. Hyper-X generates language×task adapters on one base from language embeddings; here the transfer crosses bases and vocabularies and uses parallel spans as anchors instead of language identity. No direct prior art found through 2026-09-01 under §5 coverage for adapter alignment anchored on byte spans or on cross-lingual span classes.

**Closest priors (all opened).**
1. Transport and Merge: Cross-Architecture Merging for LLMs — https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05495 — v1 2026-02-05, v2 2026-02-22 — OT on activations, neuron anchors, base-weight fusion; Qwen2.5-7B → LLaMA-3-1B (different tokenizers) with small gains.
2. Cross-Tokenizer LLM Distillation through a Byte-Level Interface (BLD) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07466 — v1 2026-04-08, AdaptFM poster (OpenReview KvUnIAPEg7) — teacher outputs converted to byte probabilities; student gets a byte decoder head; 1B–8B; "CTD is still an open problem". And ACTD — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29662 — 2026-08-30, EMNLP 2026 main — anchor loss with residual regularization for cross-tokenizer output distillation.
3. Hyper-X: A Unified Hypernetwork for Multi-Task Multilingual Transfer — https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12148 — 2022-05-24, EMNLP 2022 — single base; adapters conditioned on task and language embeddings.
4. Franken-Adapter — https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08037 — 2025-02-12 — custom vocabularies + embedding surgery for cross-lingual adaptation within a model; TokAlign++ — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13429 — 2026-05-13 — vocabulary adaptation via a learned token-alignment lexicon (neither moves task adapters).
5. Vowel Signs Are Not Letters — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26449 — 2026-08-26 — `\p{L}+` fertility floors 1.47× (Tibetan) to 9.02× (Thai) on all 17 abugidas; supplies the fertility variable and the fixed-regex control.

**Falsifiable predictions.**
- P1 (from-scratch triplet). Token-anchored retention falls by ≥ 0.15 per unit of D (log fertility ratio; up to ≈ 1.4 between BPE-32k and bytes on abugidas) across the 10 languages, while byte-span-anchored retention has slope |κ'| ≤ 0.05 and mean retention ≥ 0.60 on all three pairs.
- P2 (script contrast). Byte-span anchoring beats token anchoring by ≥ 0.15 retention on Hindi/Thai/Tamil and by ≤ 0.05 on Latin-script languages.
- P3 (frozen extension). Qwen3-0.6B-Base adapters ported onto Bolmo-1B's latent transformer with byte-span anchors recover ≥ 0.40 of fresh-LoRA lift on format/procedural tasks vs ≤ 0.15 with patch-position anchoring; on Qwen3-0.6B-Base → Qwen3.5-4B, byte-span alignment closes ≥ 50% of the gap between token-anchored retention and candidate A's iso-tokenizer operator-port retention.
- P4 (anchor, not regex). Under byte-span anchoring, swapping the `\p{L}+`-fixed tokenizer for the broken one changes retention by ≤ 0.03.

**Kill conditions.** Byte-span anchoring ≤ token anchoring on the triplet, or |κ − κ'| < 0.05 (fertility does not matter for adapter transport — PorTAL's silent tokenizer crossing is vindicated); Transport-and-Merge OT or greedy byte-prefix token alignment matches byte-span anchoring; cross-tokenizer output distillation of the adapted source (BLD/ACTD) at equal compute matches the weight port; fresh LoRA on 64 labeled target examples matches.

**Cheapest decisive pilot.** Phase 0 (CPU): build anchor classes from 20k parallel sentence pairs × 10 languages with span links; implement byte-range intersection pooling for BPE/byte/patch segmentations; deterministic ridge/Procrustes fit with gauge check; synthetic test — one model, two tokenizations of the same text, planted rotation recovered by byte-span anchoring but not by position anchoring. Phase 1 (≤ 16 H100-h): three 60M from-scratch models (≈ 2 h each, one seed in the pilot — a stated limit; 3-seed triplet is the follow-up); 4 format/procedural tasks × adapters, ports under both anchorings, fresh-LoRA oracles (≈ 3 h); Qwen3-0.6B-Base → Bolmo-1B (≈ 2.5 h) and → Qwen3.5-4B (≈ 2.5 h); baselines — Transport-and-Merge OT, greedy token alignment, BLD-style output distillation (≈ 2 h). Registry ids: qwen3-0.6b-base, qwen3.5-4b, smollm2-135m (same-family different-tokenizer sanity pair, English only), blt-1b (discovery only), plus bolmo-1b to register.

**Controls.** Token-position anchoring; greedy byte-prefix token alignment (cross-tokenizer-distillation style); Transport-and-Merge OT (2602.05495); BLD (2604.07466) / ACTD (2608.29662) output distillation as the alternative route; fresh LoRA; Cross-LoRA (2508.05232); Theseus (2602.12952; identical-tokenizer pairs only); Hyper-X-style target-native language×task adapters (labeled); parity-aware / `\p{L}+`-fixed tokenizer arm (2606.15044, 2608.26449); romanized-input arm (2608.25904); held-out prompt formats (2608.09490); iso-parameter alignment budgets; 3 seeds on adapters and alignments.
