A technical report on a hybrid architecture that interleaves softmax attention with a linear (lightning) attention variant, in mixture-of-experts models trained for long-context reasoning, argues that instability in their reinforcement learning comes from a training-inference disparity that accumulates across many modules and is worsened by expert routing and by long chain-of-thought outputs. Among the module-level fixes it names the linear-attention recurrent state, which it calls the KV cache: because the state is an accumulation, keeping it in bf16 in the inference engine makes errors accumulate progressively through the recurrence, so the state must be held at higher precision such as fp32; a figure shows token-probability scatter before and after this correction. The other fixes are an fp32 language-model head implemented with a custom matrix-multiply kernel, fp32 normalization with matched epsilon and un-fused residuals, the same attention backend in training and inference with prefill and decode aligned, matched rotary-embedding implementations, and a stable top-k with deterministic expert permutation. An ablation of long reinforcement-learning runs shows training reward improving as the fixes are applied cumulatively, the state-cache and language-model-head fix together first, then normalization, attention and rotary embeddings. Once the stack is aligned module by module, the report finds that no algorithmic correction is needed and uses the inference engine's own token probabilities as the denominator of the clipped importance ratio, instead of probabilities recomputed by the trainer, which it reports gives higher late-stage reward and a more stable disparity. The work is done on an internal training and inference stack; the state fix is not separated from the language-model-head fix, no reduced-precision format other than bf16 is tested, no importance-sampling correction is crossed with the state precision, and no seed counts or numerical effect sizes are reported.
