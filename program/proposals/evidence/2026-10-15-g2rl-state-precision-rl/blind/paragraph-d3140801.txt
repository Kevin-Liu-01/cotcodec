In hybrid language models that interleave softmax attention with gated delta-rule linear-attention layers, the inference engine that samples reinforcement-learning rollouts keeps each linear layer's recurrent state (a per-head key-value matrix) in a configurable number format and rounds it after every generated token, while the trainer recomputes the same tokens with a chunked kernel that accumulates the state in fp32. Under round-to-nearest, state updates smaller than half a unit in the last place are lost, so the rounding error is a coherent bias that builds up over decode steps instead of averaging out. On an open 4B hybrid in a stock engine, the per-token KL between sampler and trainer is flat in position with an fp32 state, about 2x larger with an fp16 state and about 9x larger with a bf16 state within a 16K-token response cap (growing further at longer lengths), and bf16-state rollouts reason systematically longer. The work treats the state's number format as a controlled, deployable off-policy lever in group-relative policy optimization: with weights, activations, trainer, data, learning-rate schedule and seeds fixed, it trains with fp32-, bf16- and fp16-state rollouts over several seeds and asks whether held-out learning under one common fp32-state evaluator, length and truncation dynamics, and stability change, and whether token-level truncated importance sampling applied to bf16 rollouts recovers the fp32 outcome. Its mechanism-level claim is that the off-policy bias has two parts, a per-token likelihood-ratio part that token weights remove and a shifted prefix (state-visitation) part that they cannot, and that the second dominates. Before any training it measures this at the start checkpoint by comparing full policy gradients from independent replicate batches drawn on the same prompts (fp32 against fp32 as the noise floor, fp32 against bf16 for the bias), with and without the truncated weights on the same bf16 batches; it also shows from measured weights that sequence-level importance sampling is infeasible and sequence-level masking degenerate at these lengths, which is why token-level truncation is the correction it tests.
