Interactive agent benchmarks are rerun several times with identical configurations and greedy decoding, and the method localizes where replicated runs first come apart. Replicated interactions are aligned so that each comparison starts from identical preceding recorded histories, and the first point of disagreement is attributed to the component that produced it: the agent model, a simulated user, the environment's outputs, or the serving system. Applied to a customer-service tool-use benchmark with five greedy replicates per task served by an open-source inference engine, the audit finds that transcripts diverge on every task and rewards vary on about a fifth of tasks, with the first discrepancy most often in the simulated user. Serving is examined by disabling prefix caching, which does not remove environment-output disagreements in one domain, and by comparing serving configurations, which leave different residual divergence; in a text-based household environment, the serving history changes trajectories and resetting the cache restores agreement.
