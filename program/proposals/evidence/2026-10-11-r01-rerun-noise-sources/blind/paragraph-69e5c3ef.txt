Benchmarks of interactive agents are rerun several times with identical settings and greedy decoding, and the method finds where the replicated runs first come apart. Replicated interactions are lined up so that each comparison starts from the same recorded history up to that point, and the first disagreement is charged to the component that produced it: the agent's model, a simulated user, the environment's responses, or the serving system. On a customer-service tool-use benchmark with five greedy replicates per task on an open-source inference engine, transcripts diverge on every task, rewards differ on about a fifth of tasks, and the first discrepancy is most often in the simulated user. Serving is probed by turning prefix caching off, which leaves some environment-response disagreements in place in one domain, and by comparing serving configurations, which leave different amounts of residual divergence; in a text household environment the serving history changes trajectories, and resetting the cache restores agreement.
