A frozen multimodal model performs in-context learning from sixteen image-text demonstrations. For each demonstration set, the change it induces in internal activations at the final query token is measured against matched counterfactual sets whose output symbols are deranged, and is decomposed into a component shared across queries plus a low-rank basis of query-specific variation. Interventions that replace the demonstrations are then compared in a factorial design: along a selection axis, a static additive shift reused for every query, an oracle conditional shift that inserts each query's recovered coefficients, or a conditional shift whose coefficients a small network predicts from the zero-shot query representation; along a realization axis, a single activation site, several attention and feed-forward sites, or modified attention logits. Families are matched in total rank and injected norm. Recovery is the fraction of the accuracy gain of explicit demonstrations over zero-shot that an intervention restores on held-out queries. Measured properties of the demonstration-induced change (its effective rank, the share of its energy common to all queries, how predictable the query-specific coefficients are from the query, how dispersed its causal support is across sites, and the gap between representational fit and behavioural recovery) are used to predict which intervention family is the cheapest one that reaches a fixed recovery threshold, with thresholds calibrated on synthetic tasks whose query dependence is controlled and then frozen before evaluation on natural visual question answering benchmarks.
