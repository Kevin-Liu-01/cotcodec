A pretrained decoder language model's in-context learning is analysed by relaxing softmax attention to linear attention. The attention output for a query token then splits into a term over the query's own preceding tokens and a term over the demonstration tokens, and the demonstration term equals a weight update applied to the query: a sum of outer products between each demonstration token's value vector and its key vector, the same form as the update a linear layer receives from gradient descent on those tokens (the dual form between attention and gradient descent). In-context learning is therefore read as implicit fine-tuning driven by meta-gradients computed in the forward pass. The reading is tested empirically on 1.3B- and 2.7B-parameter models over six classification tasks by comparing in-context learning with explicit fine-tuning of only the attention key and value projections, one step per demonstration in demonstration order: how many of the fine-tuned model's correct predictions in-context learning also gets right, the similarity of the two methods' changes to attention outputs and to attention weights, and whether both attend similarly to the demonstrations. Inspired by the analogy, a momentum-style attention variant is proposed and evaluated.
