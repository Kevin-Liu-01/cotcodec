A benchmark of about two hundred interactive tasks runs on virtual machines of a commercial desktop operating system and spans thirty applications, most of them exclusive to that system. Each task has a natural-language instruction, a snapshot-plus-script environment preparation and execution-based evaluation scripts that return a binary success. Every snapshot is duplicated in four non-English system languages, one of them a right-to-left language whose system interface and applications mirror automatically, and the instructions are machine-translated into the same languages with a round-trip check. Agents act on full-screen screenshots through a remote-desktop action space with a budget of fifteen screenshots. Six agents, from proprietary computer-use agents to small open models, are each run once per task and language, with instruction and interface language matched, and success rates are compared across languages and task categories; one agent is also run with English instructions in each localized interface. The right-to-left language shows the largest average drop relative to English, which the authors attribute to the visual complexity of its script, to the mirrored layout, or to both, without separating the two, and no confidence intervals or significance tests are reported. A separate subset measures susceptibility to deceptive pop-up windows.
