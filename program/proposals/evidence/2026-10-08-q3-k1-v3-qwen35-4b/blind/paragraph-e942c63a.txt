A frozen pretrained decoder language model whose layers alternate between a fixed-size recurrent state and, at every fourth layer, softmax attention is fitted at each softmax-attention layer with a small learned selector that scores four-token blocks of the context. The selector uses a few query projections of the current token, one pooled key per block and a gated sum of rectified dot products, and it is trained only by minimizing the KL divergence between its block-score distribution and a block aggregation of that layer's own attention (head-summed mass or within-block maximum); the backbone receives no gradient. Its training text includes some writing systems and excludes others. At a fixed budget of 1,024 of 8,192 context tokens, the selector's recall of a known evidence passage is compared with the recall of the exact top blocks of its own training target. The quantity of interest is a difference-in-differences: how much more evidence recall the selector loses than its target when the question is a human translation in a different writing system from the passage, compared with a question in the passage's own language. Recall is counted only on passage blocks that share no content token with either question, and only for questions without named-entity or number anchors, so literal matching cannot favour either side; a purely literal selector is run to check that this holds in both directions. The contrast is estimated for writing systems absent from the selector's training and, separately, for writing systems present in it, with a romanized transliteration of the same passage and same-script translated questions as descriptive comparators. Pre-registered gates require the dense model to show selection headroom on the masked passage and to answer better with the passage than without it before any difference is interpreted, and a negative result also requires the selector to keep at least half of its target's same-language headroom and no single layer or translation direction to show a large excess.
