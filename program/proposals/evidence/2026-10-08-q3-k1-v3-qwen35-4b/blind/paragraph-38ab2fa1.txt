A frozen pretrained decoder language model with grouped-query full-attention layers, including the full-attention layers of hybrid models whose other layers are linear or recurrent, is given a reference selector. For each layer and query position, dense attention is computed and averaged over heads into one token-importance distribution; the top-k tokens by that mass are kept, and attention is recomputed over only that support. The reference is not meant for deployment: it measures how much task quality is lost from the budget and the support granularity alone. A small learned selector that collapses the heads into a single scoring head is then trained only by minimizing the KL divergence to the same head-averaged attention-mass distribution, with the backbone frozen, and it replaces the reference at inference. Task quality on retrieval-heavy long-context benchmarks, at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and top-k budgets from 512 to 2,048 tokens, is compared across dense attention, the reference selector and the learned selector. The comparison separates the loss due to the budget, the additional loss due to the learned selector, and the further loss due to runtime constraints such as sharing one selection across a block of query positions.
