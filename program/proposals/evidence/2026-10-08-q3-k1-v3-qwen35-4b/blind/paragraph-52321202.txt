A frozen pretrained decoder language model, including hybrid models whose other layers are linear or recurrent, has each grouped-query full-attention layer paired with a reference token selector: at every layer and query position, dense attention is computed and averaged over heads into a single distribution over context tokens, the top-k tokens by that mass are kept, and attention is recomputed on that support alone. The reference is an instrument rather than a deployable method; it measures what the budget and the granularity of the support cost by themselves. A small learned selector with one scoring head is then trained, with the backbone frozen, only to minimize the KL divergence to the same head-averaged mass distribution, and it replaces the reference at inference. Dense attention, the reference and the learned selector are compared on task quality over retrieval-heavy long-context benchmarks at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and budgets from 512 to 2,048 tokens, so that the quality loss splits into a part due to the budget, an additional part due to the learned selector, and a further part due to runtime choices such as sharing one selection across a block of query positions.
