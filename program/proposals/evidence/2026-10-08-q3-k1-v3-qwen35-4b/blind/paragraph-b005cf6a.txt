A pretrained decoder language model is kept frozen, and its grouped-query full-attention layers, including those of hybrid models whose remaining layers are linear or recurrent, are given a reference selector. At each layer and query position the reference computes dense attention, averages it over heads into one importance distribution over tokens, keeps the top-k tokens by that mass and recomputes attention over only that support. The reference is a measuring device, not a deployable method: it shows how much task quality the budget and the granularity of the support cost on their own. A small learned selector that collapses the heads into one scoring head is then trained only by minimizing the KL divergence to the same head-averaged attention-mass distribution, with the backbone frozen, and it takes the reference's place at inference. Task quality on retrieval-heavy long-context benchmarks is compared across dense attention, the reference and the learned selector, at context lengths from 4K to 128K tokens and budgets from 512 to 2,048 tokens. The comparison separates the loss caused by the budget, the extra loss caused by the learned selector, and the further loss caused by runtime choices such as sharing one selection across a block of query positions.
