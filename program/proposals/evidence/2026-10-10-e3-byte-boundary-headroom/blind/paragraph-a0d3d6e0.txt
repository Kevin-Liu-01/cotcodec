Given multilingual language models that each use one shared subword tokenizer, the method asks how well the tokenizer's segmentations of two languages correspond, as a predictor of cross-lingual transfer. A statistical word aligner, with priors trained on a separate parallel corpus of up to 300,000 sentence pairs per language pair, is run for a single iteration on a multi-parallel, professionally translated test corpus that has been tokenized into subword tokens, so the aligned units are the tokenizer's own tokens. From the symmetrized alignment, two scores are derived for each language pair. The first is the proportion of subword tokens in the source language that have a one-to-one correspondence with tokens of the target language, taken per direction because it drops when the source language is over-segmented. The second is the aligner's estimate of the maximum unnormalized log-probability of the links in its last sampling iteration, averaged over both directions. Both scores are compared with distributional token-overlap measures by rank correlation against downstream cross-lingual transfer (classification and tagging tasks fine-tuned in one language and evaluated in another) and against sentence-retrieval accuracy and embedding-margin measures of cross-lingual representation alignment. The comparison spans tokenizers of different types, encoder and decoder models, and language pairs written in the same script and in different scripts. The aligner score predicts transfer better than token overlap, especially for pairs in different scripts, and is put forward as a criterion for building fairer multilingual tokenizers.
