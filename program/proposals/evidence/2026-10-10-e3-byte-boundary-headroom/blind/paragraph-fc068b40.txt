Given a professionally translated test set (English sentences and their translations into a second language) and released byte-level language models whose learned boundary predictor scores, at every byte, how likely a new chunk is to start there, the method asks whether the model's strongest boundaries fall at corresponding places in a sentence and its translation once word segmentation and boundary rate are taken out. Each side is split into words by a fixed monolingual segmenter, and every boundary score is mapped to a canonical character gap and from there to the gaps between words. Two word aligners from different families are each run once on the word-segmented pair, and only the links both produce define the target: a pair of word gaps, one per language, is allowed when splitting some alignment-consistent phrase pair at those two gaps leaves two consistent sub-pairs, in straight or in inverted order, so nested and reordered phrase structure counts. Each sentence gets one common budget k, half of the smaller side's word gaps capped by the largest number of disjoint allowed pairs. On each side the model's k highest-scoring word gaps are selected, and the hits are the largest number of disjoint allowed pairs among the selected gaps. The statistic is chance-corrected agreement scaled by its maximum, S = (hits - expected hits of k word gaps drawn at random on each side) / (k - that expectation), pooled over sentences. Because selection is confined to word gaps, a system that only knows where words end scores zero; because k never exceeds half of either side's word gaps or the number of allowed pairs, the score cannot saturate and its maximum is exactly one. Aligner error lowers S multiplicatively: the attenuation is bounded by simulation from the aligners' published error rates and their measured agreement, or measured on a small gold-aligned sample and divided out. A pre-registered rule on a document-cluster bootstrap interval returns stop (the strongest boundaries already correspond nearly perfectly), headroom, or no verdict, and a monolingual syntactic reference selected the same way tests whether any shortfall could be closed without translations. No model is trained; the output is a go or no-go for building translation-supervised boundary training.
