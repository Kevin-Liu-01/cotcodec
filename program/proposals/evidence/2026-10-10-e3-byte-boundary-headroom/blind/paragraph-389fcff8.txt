Given multilingual language models that each use one shared subword tokenizer, the method measures how well the tokenizer's segmentations of two languages correspond, as a predictor of cross-lingual transfer. A statistical word aligner, with priors estimated from a separate parallel corpus of up to 300,000 sentence pairs per language pair, is run for one iteration on a multi-parallel, professionally translated test set that has already been split into the tokenizer's subword tokens, so the units being aligned are the units being evaluated. Two scores are read from the symmetrized alignment for each language pair: the share of source-language tokens that have a one-to-one correspondence with target-language tokens, taken per direction because it falls when the source is over-segmented, and the aligner's own estimate of the maximum unnormalized log-probability of its links in the last sampling iteration, averaged over both directions. Neither score is set against a chance level or against a maximum at a fixed number of units. The scores are compared with distributional token-overlap measures by rank correlation against downstream cross-lingual transfer (classification and sequence labelling fine-tuned in one language and tested in another) and against sentence retrieval and embedding-alignment measures, across tokenizer types, encoder and decoder models, and pairs in the same script and in different scripts. The aligner score predicts transfer better than token overlap, especially across scripts, and is proposed as a criterion for building fairer multilingual tokenizers.
