Decoder-only language models are trained with weights, activations and gradients quantized to a 4-bit floating-point grid with two exponent bits and one mantissa bit in all three matrix products of each linear layer. To choose the block-scaled format, a mid-sized model is trained with blocks of 16 elements under seven 8-bit scale encodings spanning one to eight exponent bits, all at the same storage, and block sizes from 8 to 128 are compared under a power-of-two scale and under an 8-bit floating-point scale with four exponent and three mantissa bits. Stochastic rounding is switched on separately for each of the six quantized operands of the three products. The scale encoding matters strongly: the narrowest-range encoding diverges, and the encodings with four and three exponent bits are best. Block size matters modestly, with diminishing returns below 16 elements. Stochastic rounding helps only on gradients in the backward and update products and on activations in the update product. The study adopts blocks of 16 with the four-exponent-bit scale, round-to-nearest in the forward pass and stochastic rounding for gradients. A theoretical analysis of quantized gradient descent with stochastic rounding identifies a threshold beyond which quantized training stops making progress: a gradient norm below about the square root of three times the quantization noise. That analysis motivates a short higher-precision fine-tuning phase at the end. The resulting recipe trains a multi-billion-parameter model on about a trillion tokens to near-baseline downstream accuracy. Each format sweep is a single training run, and the element grid is the same in every run.
