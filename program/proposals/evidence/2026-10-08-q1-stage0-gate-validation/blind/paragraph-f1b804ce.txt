Three correctness checkers for GPU kernels, each a superset of the previous one, are measured against an independent audit before any of them is used as a training reward. The weakest compares the candidate with the reference model on five random inputs at the benchmark's fixed shapes, at an absolute and relative tolerance of 1e-2. The second adds launch-path evidence: a hook must see a compiled kernel launch in both inference and gradient modes, and a profiler must attribute device time to a captured kernel. The third adds hidden input scalings, shape variation and unaligned remainder shapes at a tolerance of 1e-3. The kernels measured are compiler-generated and older human-written Triton kernels, deterministic single-site mutants of them produced by fixed rules, and wrapper controls that imitate documented reward hacks. Ground truth is an audit whose inputs no checker sees: a float64 oracle whose threshold is a fixed multiple of the reference's own float32 error, held-out value distributions, held-out shapes with refusal classification, and tolerance-free contracts (determinism, aliasing, output invariance under poisoned allocations, a memory sanitizer). For each checker the study reports the share of audit-rejected mutants it accepts, the share of audit-accepted correct kernels it rejects counted over independent problems, paired differences between checkers on a common kernel set, and GPU-seconds per kernel.
