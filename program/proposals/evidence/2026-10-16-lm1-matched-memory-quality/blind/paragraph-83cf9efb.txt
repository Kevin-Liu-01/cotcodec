To deploy an ultra-large mixture-of-experts language model (several hundred billion parameters) on an edge laptop with a strict 128 GB memory limit, combine three compression steps: expert pruning that removes about a quarter of the experts in each layer, guided by activation statistics on calibration data; activation adjustments that reduce the number of experts used per token and suppress routing to removed experts; and mixed-precision post-training quantization at an average below 2 bits per weight, allocated with an importance matrix. The result is a model of about 100 GB that runs at over 5 tokens per second on the laptop. Its quality is compared with uniform low-bit quantizations of the same model (a 1-bit variant at 140 GB and a 2-bit variant at 230 GB) on standard multiple-choice, commonsense and grade-school math benchmarks, where the compressed model is more accurate than the uniform 1-bit version and close to the 2-bit one at about half its size. In one further comparison, a reasoning variant of the same large model compressed to about 210 GB is compared on three reasoning benchmarks with a 235-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model from another family stored at 8 bits (about 233 GB), and it scores equal or higher on each.
