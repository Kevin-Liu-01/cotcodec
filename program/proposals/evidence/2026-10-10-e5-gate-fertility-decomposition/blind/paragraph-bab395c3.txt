Two pretrained language models without attention, whose only memory is a fixed-size recurrent state updated by a gated delta rule (one with a scalar decay per head and a short convolution, one with a decay vector per channel and an in-context removal term), read English episodes: K facts binding a noun phrase to a random 4-digit code, a 512-token passage of encyclopedia text, and a query. Recall is scored by forced choice among four codes that all occur in the episode, from their string likelihoods. Only the passage is re-tokenized, into 2.7 (or 2.0) times as many tokens, by splitting its tokens into smaller in-vocabulary pieces, so its bytes are unchanged while the number of recurrent updates grows. The load is the smallest of 4, 8 and 16 facts at which canonical recall is at most 90% on held-out episodes. On paired episodes a two-by-two design crosses the passage's tokens (canonical or re-tokenized) with its decay mass at every layer and head or channel: the re-tokenized pieces are rescaled so each canonical token's summed log-decay equals its canonical value, and the canonical tokens are given the summed log-decay their pieces received in the re-tokenized run. Restoring canonical decay at the re-tokenized writes measures the most a per-token decay-parity correction could recover; adding the extra decay at canonical writes measures the pure decay cost; their difference is the decay-by-write interaction, and with the remaining write effect the three sum exactly to the total cost. Each is read per unit of log token ratio on a guessing-corrected scale against a preset line of 3 points; a kill needs both decay contrasts below the line and summed piece decay at least 1.5 times canonical. Secondary arms silence the passage's writes, multiply canonical decay uniformly by the token ratio, lengthen the passage with more canonical text, and halve every decay rate.
