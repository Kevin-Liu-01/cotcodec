Small fixed-state recurrent cells (a diagonal-decay cell, a rank-one delta-rule cell with and without a learned decay, a vector-decay cell with a rank-one removal term, a selective state-space reference, and attention as a control) are trained from scratch under a matched state budget on synthetic masked multi-query associative recall, toggling one ingredient at a time: a short causal convolution, the transition structure (rank-one against diagonal), and decay. Recall is measured over many seeds as the number of stored pairs grows to 32, and on a haystack task in which 4 pairs are followed by distractor tokens at several sequence lengths. Decay is ablated in training, by comparing cells trained with and without it, not intervened on in a trained model; it shows no measurable mean recall cost, while the convolution accounts for most of the recall gap. The haystack failure is total and flat in length; it is attributed to interference under sparse supervision rather than to decay over distance, and a distance curriculum that changes only the training signal removes it.
