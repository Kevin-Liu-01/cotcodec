Two pretrained language models without attention, whose only memory is a fixed-size recurrent state updated by a gated delta rule (one with a scalar decay per head and a short convolution, one with a decay vector per channel and an in-context removal term), read English episodes: K facts binding a noun phrase to a random 4-digit code, a 512-token passage of encyclopedia text, and a query; recall is scored by forced choice among four candidate codes from their string likelihoods. The passage is re-tokenized into 1.5, 2.0 or 2.7 times as many tokens by splitting each canonical token into smaller in-vocabulary pieces, so its bytes and content are unchanged while the number of recurrent updates grows; facts, query and codes keep their canonical tokens. In a paired arm, the per-position decay of the re-tokenized passage is rescaled at every layer and head (or channel) so that the summed log-decay over each canonical token's pieces equals the log-decay that token received in the canonical run of the same episode, leaving the extra writes and erasures, the convolution or token shift and the unfamiliar segmentation as they are. Recall in this clamped run minus recall in the native re-tokenized run is read as the part of the re-tokenization cost carried by the extra decay mass, per unit of log token ratio, against a preset line of 3 points at loads of 1 and 4 facts; the remainder is attributed to everything else. Comparisons include a ladder that lengthens the passage with more canonical text to the same token counts, a same-count boundary perturbation, a passage whose writes are silenced, re-tokenized facts, a per-token ledger of summed piece decay against canonical decay, and a uniform halving of every decay rate.
