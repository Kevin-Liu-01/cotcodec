Pretrained linear-attention language models with a fixed-size recurrent state (data-dependent multiplicative gating, with or without a delta-rule update, at a small and a medium size), plus hybrids that add full-attention layers, read controlled associative-recall inputs: one or more key-value bindings embedded in natural-text passages from a public encyclopedia corpus, followed by a query; recall is scored among four balanced candidate values by conditional likelihood, with the target logit margin as a continuous measure and source passages as the statistical unit. The state at any position is decomposed exactly into the contributions of earlier positions, each transformed by all later transitions, which separates retention from storage and access. Causal interventions patch states, writes and query-time read components between paired donor and recipient inputs that differ only in the bound value, to locate where a fact enters the state and where it is read. Memory load (the number of active bindings at fixed sequence length) is varied separately from elapsed context (natural filler tokens between the fact and the query at fixed load), and later writes have their keys aligned with or orthogonalized against the target's write direction to test whether interference depends on overlap; retention, targeted editability and collateral damage are tracked as load grows. In hybrids the same interventions show that recall is carried by the attention cache rather than the recurrent state.
