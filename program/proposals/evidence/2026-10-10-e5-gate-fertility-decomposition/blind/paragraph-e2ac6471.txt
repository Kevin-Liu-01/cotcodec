Pretrained recurrent language models whose state is a fixed-size matrix updated by data-dependent multiplicative gating, with or without a delta-rule correction (two small models and one medium model), plus hybrids that interleave full-attention layers, read controlled associative-recall inputs: key-value bindings embedded in natural passages from a public encyclopedia corpus, followed by a query. Recall is scored among four balanced candidate values by conditional likelihood, with the target logit margin as a continuous measure and source passages as the statistical unit. Along a fixed forward trajectory the state is written exactly as a sum of earlier positions' contributions, each carried through all later transitions, which separates storage, retention and access. Paired donor and recipient inputs that differ only in the bound value support patching of the write at the fact, of the pre-query state and of per-head query-time read outputs, locating where a fact enters the state and where it is read out. Memory load (the number of active bindings at fixed length) is varied separately from elapsed context (natural filler between fact and query at fixed load); later write keys are aligned with or orthogonalized against the target's write direction to test overlap-dependent interference; and recall, targeted editability and collateral damage are tracked as load grows. In the hybrids the same interventions locate recall in the attention cache rather than the recurrent state.
