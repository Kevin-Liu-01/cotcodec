A small hybrid language model, in which nine linear-recurrent layers with a gated delta-rule matrix state are interleaved with three sliding-window attention layers of window 512, is trained from scratch on one billion tokens of four languages using next-token prediction only: three quarters monolingual text, one fifth translation pairs presented as two sequences that share a bitwise-identical preceding context, and one twentieth a same-language curriculum of facts (a natural sentence followed by a four-digit code) that are queried later in the sequence. The model is then probed with prompts that state eight such facts and later ask for one fact's code by giving its sentence, either repeated verbatim or as a translation that shares no surface token with any stated sentence. In the main condition every fact lies farther back than the largest distance the attention layers and short convolutions can bridge without the recurrent state (1,560 positions by construction, checked by perturbation), so a correct answer must have been carried by the recurrent state. The same checkpoint is also read with the queried fact inside one attention window, in the range reachable only by relaying through several attention layers, with the recurrent state reset between the facts and the query, and with the queried fact's writes into the state removed. Exact match on the code is judged against a one-in-eight ceiling for answers that copy an in-context code without matching the sentence, through a binding margin (target hits minus distractor hits). Each of three seeds is classified against fixed lines (in-language recall at least 60%, cross-language at least 15%) with intervals clustered by sentence, and seeds that fall on both sides of a line are reported as unreliability. Passing only licenses a later study of an auxiliary objective that makes the recurrent-state write for a sentence match the write for its translation; this step trains no such objective.
