Two pretrained hybrid language models of about four billion parameters, which interleave linear-recurrent layers (a gated delta-rule state in one, a selective state-space state in the other) with full-attention layers, are studied on long-context question answering (about 8,200 test examples with an average context of about 84,000 tokens) and on long-horizon text-based agent tasks. Each input is divided into a past portion and a current portion, for example the context and the question, and the pathway that may carry past information into the current portion is restricted in two complementary ways. In the recurrent-only condition, attention from the current portion to the past portion is masked while the recurrent state propagates normally, so past information reaches the current portion only through the recurrent state. In the attention-only condition, causal attention is unchanged while the recurrent state is reset at the boundary, so past information crosses the boundary only through attention. The share of full accuracy retained under each condition shows that the models rely far more on attention than on the recurrent state, and standard supervised fine-tuning raises attention-only accuracy while leaving recurrent-only accuracy almost unchanged. The proposed remedy adds to the supervised next-token loss an auxiliary loss computed on an extra forward pass in which attention to the preceding context is masked while the recurrent layers stay unrestricted, so that information from earlier context must travel through recurrent memory. The two losses are weighted and optimized jointly with low-rank adapters, with the number of optimization steps halved to match compute. This raises question-answering accuracy (from 34.6% to 38.5% on one model) and recurrent-only accuracy (from 5.0% to 22.8%), with gains concentrated on longer contexts and on questions that need aggregation. All tasks are in one language.
