Hybrid language models that interleave sliding-window softmax attention with a linear-recurrent memory layer (an extended long short-term memory variant) are trained from scratch at 1.4 and 7 billion parameters on 150 billion tokens, with 16,000-token training sequences and window sizes from 128 to 2,048. The receptive field of stacked window layers grows with depth times window, and needle-in-a-haystack retrieval is evaluated far beyond that range (up to 131,000 tokens), where retrieval must rely on the recurrent memory. Counter to intuition, shorter training windows give better long-range retrieval: with a long window most training dependencies fall inside attention, so the recurrent memory is under-trained. A grid of training-time against test-time window sizes separates the two effects, and a stochastic procedure that switches between a short and a long window from batch to batch recovers both short-context quality and long-range retrieval. All evaluations are in one language, and the pathways are separated only through the window sizes used at training and test time.
