Two pretrained hybrid language models of about four billion parameters, which interleave linear-recurrent layers (a gated delta-rule state in one, a selective state-space state in the other) with full-attention layers, are studied on long-context question answering (average context about 84,000 tokens) and on long-horizon text-based agent tasks. To measure which pathway carries past information, the input is divided into segments and two complementary conditions are run. In the recurrent-only condition, attention is blocked across segment boundaries while the recurrent state propagates through the whole sequence. In the attention-only condition, causal attention is unchanged while the recurrent state is reset at every segment boundary. The accuracy retained under each condition shows a much greater reliance on attention than on the recurrent state, and standard supervised fine-tuning raises attention-only accuracy while leaving recurrent-only accuracy almost unchanged. The proposed remedy adds to the supervised next-token loss an auxiliary loss computed on an extra forward pass in which attention to the preceding context is masked while the recurrent layers stay unrestricted, so that information from earlier context can reach the answer only through recurrent memory. The two losses are weighted and optimized jointly with low-rank adapters, with the number of optimization steps halved to match compute. This raises average question-answering accuracy (34.6% to 38.5% on one model) and recurrent-only accuracy (5.0% to 22.8%), with gains concentrated on longer contexts and on questions that need aggregation. All tasks are in a single language.
