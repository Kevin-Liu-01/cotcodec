An open-source fork of a widely used GGUF inference engine adds SSD expert streaming for mixture-of-experts models on Apple laptops. Routed experts are extracted from the model file into a per-layer side file and served through a fixed-size per-layer slot bank with least-recently-used refill and an optional temporal prefetch. The engine can write a JSONL trace of the experts selected at each layer call. For a one-trillion-parameter model with 384 routed experts per layer and eight selected per token (a roughly 1.9-bit file, 217 GB of extracted experts), the author reports measurements on a 128 GB laptop of the current generation: one greedy prompt, 100 generated tokens, from a cold start, and mostly with routing reduced to four experts per token. At four experts, decode runs at 3.2-3.3 tokens/s for banks of 16 to 256 slots per layer, with reported per-call hit rates rising from 55.6% to 76.4% and saturating at 128 slots. At the native eight experts with 64 slots per layer, decode is 2.2 tokens/s at a 67.2% hit rate. Reads run at about 4.7 GB/s per 3.28 MB read. A companion workflow document prescribes how to estimate the benefit of a persistent expert cache for a new model:
- define the routed expert unit from exact file manifests;
- compute the resident-byte cost of per-layer banks;
- collect routing traces under realistic workloads (its worked example is one prompt generated to 12,000 tokens with end-of-sequence ignored);
- summarise unique experts per layer, expert frequencies, misses and bytes per token;
- simulate a uniform per-layer static bank, a global static set under a byte budget, and a refillable least-recently-used cache;
- convert the simulated misses into tokens per second by calibrating against runtime anchors from the same engine: the baseline streamed speed, the banked speed and an oracle all-hit replay.
The document publishes no estimator outputs, no comparison against independent measurements of other models or held-out conditions, and no sessions beyond its single prompt.
