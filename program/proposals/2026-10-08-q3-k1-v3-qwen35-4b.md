## Claim and Research Question

The experiment under review is the draft registration
`program/preregistrations/q3-k1-localization-screen-v3.md` (new experiment
id `q3-k1-localization-screen-v3`, status DRAFT). It is the K1 v3 that program
decision D26 called for after K1 v2's honest gauntlet exit (score 45, triad 3
of 3 refuted). Its base is the one the dense pre-check v2 licensed:
Qwen3.5-4B-Base at revision `1001bb4d`, combined read NEGATIVE_CAPABLE_V3
(E1). This proposal argues for that design and states its defects. The
registration carries all seven requirements of the combined read and answers
the independent verifier's three caveats; the traceability table at the end
of the registration maps each one to a clause.

Registered question (v3, "Question"): on frozen Qwen3.5-4B-Base, do
block-form (compress ratio 4) indexers fitted on its eight softmax-attention
layers, each distilled by KL from that layer's own attention, lose more
needle-selection recall than the dense block top-k of their own distillation
target when the question is a human translation in a different script from
the needle passage, compared with a question in the needle's own language?
Recall counts only needle blocks that share no content token with either
question (overlap-masked recall), on entity-unanchored questions, at 1,024 of
8,192 tokens. The primary stratum is the seven scripts indexer training never
saw. The comparator stratum is three scripts it did see (th, hi, km).

Variable and statistic. For target T in {hs (head-sum), mp (QSA max-pool)},
xi^M_T is the macro over the 14 unseen-stratum pairs of the controlled-family
mean of [R^M_ind(MN) − R^M_ind(CX)] − [R^M_T(MN) − R^M_T(CX)] in recall
points. R^M is masked recall, R_ind the seed-averaged indexer at the frozen
learning rate, R_T the dense top-256 blocks of its own target, MN the question
in the needle's language and CX the human-translated question in the other
language. The co-statistic is xi_rel^M_T, the macro of G^M(MN) − G^M(CX) with
G^M(c) = (R^M_ind(c) − R^M_rand(c)) / (R^M_T(c) − R^M_rand(c)).

Claim scope: **attachment-capability.** The indexers are trained KL-only on a
frozen backbone. That is the Stage-1 state that QSA reports degrades quality
until joint sparse training (C04), and production indexers are co-trained
(C05, C14). No outcome is a statement about production DSA or QSA indexers,
and none is an architecture claim (rule 8).

What this proposal claims:

1. The screen measures one quantity that no source in the stated coverage
   reports for any model: the excess evidence-recall loss of a KL-distilled
   in-model selector over its own dense target under a cross-script question.
2. On Qwen3.5-4B-Base the registered design can reach both decisive verdicts.
   GO and NEGATIVE are identified against the three confounds that sank K1 v2
   (literal overlap, a flat selector, script exposure) and against the three
   that sank wave 1 of this gauntlet (a literal check blind to a positive
   channel, a one-sided NEGATIVE region, an unmeasured seed variance), to the
   extent stated and simulated below.
3. The cheapest decisive steps run first: zero-GPU bundle facts, a dense-only
   development pre-step, and a dense-only H2 gate on the audit partition.
   Indexers are trained only after those pass.

What it does not claim: that a decisive verdict is likely. The repair's
simulation and the synthesis owner's judgement (Mechanism section, outcome
map) put the unconditional probability of GO or NEGATIVE at about
0.1 to 0.3, and its likeliest single stop is still the H2 gate. It also does
not claim that overlap masking removes every lexical channel. Masking removes
shared tokens from the measured evidence; literal matches elsewhere still
compete for the budget and carry into the blocks after them. The repair
measures both channels with two literal-free evidence sets and conditions both
verdicts on them, with a bound derived in a block-level simulation (E12), not
proven for the real model.

Decision this proposal serves: admission. The repaired synthesis projection of
the registered caps is 6.94 GPU-h central and 9.46 high (E15; wave 1 7.21 and
9.79). Under D24 any total over 8 GPU-h waits on Kevin, and D26 already
reserves the K1 v3 decision for him. Kevin can admit v3 as drafted, admit it
with owner options (decision 64: 2,000 primary-partition cells for dense-only
H2; decision 81: five seeds; the CS legs), cut descriptive legs, or stop Q3.

## Strategic Fit and Why Now

- The dense pre-check v2 changed what K1 can answer (E1). On Qwen3-0.6B-Base
  a NEGATIVE was out of reach (H1_CX 12.25 points). On Qwen3.5-4B-Base the
  dense target's cross-script selection headroom is 42.15 points (99 percent
  interval 35.76 to 48.20), and its own same-language advantage is only 2.23
  points. So a selector's excess loss has room to show, and so does its
  absence.
- The base is now the exact setting of the most direct apparatus prior.
  SpotAttention fits a KL-distilled lightning-indexer-style selector to every
  full-attention layer of frozen Qwen3.5-4B. It trains on English FineWeb-Edu
  only, and its Limitations say non-English data is untested (C02).
  Oracle-Guided Sparse Prefill distils frozen KL indexers on Qwen3.5 hybrid
  full-attention layers. Its section 9.1 names "distilled-indexer recall of
  oracle support" as the most direct next step, still undone as of 2026-10-08
  (C01, C38).
- Production placement matches the 4B layout. QSA puts its indexer on the
  softmax layers, "every fourth in this hybrid" (C04). On Qwen3.5-4B exact
  retrieval survives only through attention (C12), so an indexer on the eight
  softmax layers sits exactly where the model retrieves.
- Hybrid multilinguality is organized by layer. Qwen3.5 is less
  cross-lingually aligned than Qwen3 in 10 of 13 languages, and the largest
  cross-lingual event is at the first full-attention layer (C08). The 4B
  target's own MN-minus-CX gap is concentrated at layers 3 and 31 (E5). This
  is why v3 adds per-layer reporting and a per-layer veto on NEGATIVE.
- Program fit, stated plainly. Q3 is backfill behind Q1 and Q2; Q1 is closed
  (D46). K1 v3's projected caps, 6.94 to 9.46 GPU-h after the repair, are
  about K1 v2's 8.05 and five to six times the Q3 file's original K1 estimate
  of 1.5 GPU-h. The H2 gate is the likeliest stop, and it ends the screen
  after about 1.2 to 1.6 GPU-h used.
- Why now: the dense pre-check gave the go-ahead to design (D26 asked for it),
  and the lane-862 code path is the only measured 4B evaluation path. The
  window is not urgent: no concurrent paper runs the measurement (C38, C11).

Corrections against the cells, made by synthesis:

1. The frontier cell called the Belebele romanized variants a
   "same-language, same-wording, cross-script condition". The cross-domain
   cell's row check (C16) shows that only the passages are transliterations;
   the romanized questions are written separately. The registered TR leg
   therefore changes only the needle (decision 57).
2. The cost cell proposed a one-sided gate on the masked literal selector
   (masked LEX xi_rel at most 0.05). Masking turns the MN question's
   same-language haystack matches into distractors that a literal-leaning
   selector over-selects on MN only. Under masking the literal bias can
   therefore go either way, so wave 1 registered the gate two-sided: |xi| at
   most 2.5 and |xi_rel| at most 0.05. Wave 1's refuters then showed that the
   gate itself was one-sided by construction (LEX and the mask share one
   token rule), and the repair replaces it (Changes after wave 1, item 1).
3. The cost cell kept re-tokenized proxy contexts to preserve the
   development-artifact gate. The lane-862 receipt shows that this choice
   gives language-dependent context lengths: matched budgets fall in clusters
   from 107 to 327 blocks, some contexts are longer than the indexer's
   training length, and length correlates with language (E6). Synthesis
   registers native 8,192-token contexts instead, and keeps a validity gate
   by re-evaluating 48 units of the lane-862 artifact in the smoke
   (decisions 50 and 51).
4. The cells' summary counts of query types disagree with their own logs. The
   counts in this proposal come from the logs (frontier 42 keyword, 6
   embedding, 4 OpenAlex; cross-domain 29, 7, 17).

The three closest works:

1. **Oracle-Guided Sparse Prefill** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07703 , Wang
   et al., v1 2026-06-05) is the closest mechanism and the prior used for
   blind discrimination.
   - Shared with K1 v3: a frozen backbone, here including Qwen3.5 hybrid
     full-attention layers; a reference top-k selector built from dense
     attention mass; a learned selector KL-distilled to the same distribution;
     and the separation of selector error from what the budget alone loses.
   - Delta: K1 v3 measures per-prompt evidence-selection recall, not task
     quality. Its contrast is cross-script against same-language questions,
     as a difference-in-differences against the selector's own target, on
     overlap-masked evidence and entity-controlled questions, split by whether
     indexer training saw the script. It adds literal, random and null
     references and dense headroom and answering gates.
   - The paper's own section 9.1 names the monolingual version of this
     measurement (indexer recall of oracle support) as its next step. The
     novelty refuter in K1 v2 used exactly that to argue trivial recombination
     (about 0.6), and the argument is stronger now that the base is a
     Qwen3.5 hybrid.
   - Anonymized mechanism paragraphs are in the bundle (`blind/`).
2. **SpotAttention** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22874 , Ahmad and Yun, v1
   2026-06-22) is the direct apparatus prior on this exact base.
   - What it does: a KL-distilled selector on every full-attention layer of
     frozen Qwen3.5-4B, trained on English only, with parity at static budgets
     of 50 and 75 percent.
   - Delta: K1 v3's object is the selector's recall against its own target
     across scripts at a 12.5 percent budget. SpotAttention measures task
     parity in English at 50 to 75 percent.
   - Its "non-English data is untested" is the gap. Its English-only training
     stream would be the natural unseen-language baseline arm, which v3 does
     not register (decision 72).
   - A supplementary blind packet is in the bundle.
3. **Lost in Compression** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26175 , Lukauskas, v1
   2026-07-27) is the closest cross-lingual selector audit, and it supplies
   K1 v3's audit form.
   - What it does: Belebele in ten languages, budgets matched in the target
     tokenizer, out-of-model compressors, questions always in the context's
     language. Accuracy is normalised between a no-context and a full-context
     anchor into U, the fraction of usable context value retained, and the
     transfer gap is U(EN) − U(language). English-supervised compressors show
     the gap and a multilingually supervised one does not, so supervision
     language, not architecture, drives it.
   - Structural identity, stated plainly (the wave-1 ledger left it out):
     K1 v3's G^M(c) = (R_ind − R_rand) / (R_T − R_rand) is U with the
     selector's own dense target as the ceiling and the random selector as
     the floor; xi_rel = G(MN) − G(CX) is a transfer gap across question
     languages; Delta_xi (unseen minus seen scripts) is LiC's
     supervision-language contrast made within one model.
   - Delta: K1 v3's selector is inside the model (LiC excludes KV-cache
     eviction because it needs model internals), the ceiling is the
     selector's own distillation target rather than the full context, the
     outcome is evidence recall rather than task accuracy, the contrast is the
     question's language at a fixed passage language rather than the
     passage's language, and the primary statistic is a difference in
     differences in recall points, with the literal-free and pre-literal
     versions and the direction decomposition that LiC has no analogue for
     (it has no cross-language question).
   - Its rate dependence and its finding that the gap follows supervision
     language predict a small xi for K1's multilingually trained indexers.
   - A blind packet built from it is in the bundle for this run's critic
     (the wave-1 novelty refuter's request).

## Mechanism and Falsifiable Predictions

Mechanism statement. This is the paragraph given to this run's blind critic,
anonymized (`blind/paragraph-0ccbe84a.txt`, text sha256 `0ccbe84a...`; wave 1's
paragraph `e942c63a` stays in the bundle as history):

A frozen pretrained decoder language model whose layers alternate between a
fixed-size recurrent state and, at every fourth layer, softmax attention is
fitted at each softmax-attention layer with a small learned selector that
scores four-token blocks of the context from a few query projections of the
current token and one pooled key per block. Each selector is trained only by
minimizing the KL divergence between its block distribution and a block
aggregation of its own layer's attention (head-summed mass or within-block
maximum); the backbone receives no gradient, and the selectors' training text
covers some writing systems and not others. At a budget of 1,024 of 8,192
context tokens, a selector's recall of a known evidence passage is compared
with the recall of the exact top blocks of its own training target, as a
difference-in-differences: how much more recall the selector loses than its
target when the question is a human translation in a different writing system
from the passage than when it is in the passage's language. Averaging the two
translation directions isolates the mismatch between question and passage
scripts; half their difference isolates a deficit tied to the question's
script, and a deficit tied to the passage's script cancels. Evidence counts
only passage blocks sharing no content token with either question, for
questions without named-entity or number anchors. Because literal matches can
still act on the same-language question, through competition for the budget
and through the blocks that follow a match, the contrast is recomputed on two
literal-free evidence sets: one that removes from both evidence and candidates
every block within two blocks of an exact or near-literal match, and one
restricted to passage text before the first match, which a causal model cannot
contextualize with it; a selector that scores matches and the blocks after
them is the positive control. A negative result needs the cross-script excess,
its question-script part, the excess for writing systems seen in training,
both literal-free versions and every layer with enough headroom to lie inside
a two-sided band, with seed variance estimated layer by layer from
independently initialized selectors; a positive result needs the excess to
survive on the literal-free evidence. Gates require the dense model to show
headroom on the masked passage and to answer better with it than without it
before any selector is trained.

Hypothesis under test (H_loc). A low-rank selector fitted to aggregated
attention reproduces its target's ranking where question and passage share a
language. It fails to reproduce the target's non-literal cross-script
matching, so the mismatch component of the excess is positive: xi^M_T is
greater than 0. The null is xi^M_T = 0: the selector inherits the target's
cross-script gap and adds nothing to it.

What the statistic identifies (new in the repair). Write the indexer's excess
loss over its target, in recall points, as an additive function of the
question's script and the passage's script: gamma when they differ (the
mismatch), alpha when the question is in a script unseen in indexer training,
beta when the passage is. The English-needle direction reads
alpha + gamma (its MN leg is English question, English passage, English
haystack); the X-needle direction reads gamma − alpha (its MN leg is wholly in
the unseen script). So the macro xi^M_T is gamma, the H_loc estimand; half the
difference of the directions is alpha; beta cancels inside every family and is
reported through G^M(MN) by direction. Wave 1 averaged the directions and
bounded only their points from above, so an alpha of either sign could cancel
across directions into a NEGATIVE (identification refuter).

Mechanisms that could produce an excess, separated descriptively (never by a
decision rule except where a NEGATIVE needs them all small):

- M1, capacity or subspace: four 128-dimensional query heads cannot carry the
  part of a 16-head, 256-dimensional attention that does cross-script
  matching. Predicts gamma above 0 in both strata (label SEEN_TOO).
- M2q, query-side shift: question rows in a script absent from indexer
  training are out of distribution for its query projections. Predicts alpha
  above 0: opposite-signed directions (label QUERY_SCRIPT), larger in the
  unseen stratum.
- M2k, key-side shift: passage keys in an unseen script are out of
  distribution. Predicts beta above 0, which cancels in xi; the TR leg moves
  bn's and hi's needles into Latin at fixed language and content and is the
  key-side test.
- M3, layer position, specific to the hybrid: cross-lingual matching
  concentrates at particular softmax layers (C08, E5). Predicts a
  layer-concentrated excess, which the per-layer table and the per-layer
  NEGATIVE veto expose.

### The literal check and its bound

Why wave 1's check was blind. The overlap mask and the literal selector LEX
used one token rule, so every block LEX could score was masked. Masked LEX
recall was therefore its random fill minus what same-language haystack
matches took from the budget: its masked xi could only be negative, whatever
the indexer did. Simulation S1 (E12) confirms it: LEX's masked xi is negative
in all eighteen scenarios, −0.85 to −5.65 points, while the kernel-literal
selector LEXk, whose rule differs from the mask, reads +0.22 to +2.53 except
with a weakly literal target and a dense literal haystack (−2.5, displacement
dominating). That is why the registered positive controls test what LEXk
does to the spill profile and to LF's candidates, not the sign of its xi.

The channels. Masking removes shared tokens from the evidence. It leaves two
literal channels that act on the MN leg only, because CX shares almost no
token with the passage (E2):

- displacement: literal blocks in the same-language haystack, and the masked
  needle blocks, attract a literal-leaning selector's budget, which lowers
  its masked recall on MN and biases xi downward;
- spill: the blocks that follow a literal match carry its identity into their
  keys (the hybrid's short convolution and recurrent state, previous-token
  and induction heads, C47), so a literal-leaning selector raises its masked
  recall of unmasked needle blocks after a match on MN and biases xi upward;
  at long range the same mechanism also displaces the budget toward haystack
  blocks after haystack matches.

The instruments. LF removes from both the evidence and the candidates every
block within two blocks of an exact or near-literal match of the leg's
question: displacement by literal blocks and their neighbours and local spill
are outside it by construction. PRE keeps only passage text before the first
literal match of either question: the question comes after the passage and
the model is causal, so no key in PRE can carry a later match of the
passage's own.

The derived bound. Write xi^M = gamma + b + e, xi^LF = kappa_LF gamma + b_LF + e'
and xi^PRE = kappa_PRE gamma + b_PRE + e'', with b the literal bias of each
statistic and kappa its share of the true excess (1 when the excess is spread
evenly over the passage). S1 is a block-level model of the two legs (2,048
blocks, budget 256, target masked headroom 32 to 40 points), with haystack
literal density 15, 40 or 80 blocks, spill range 0.5, 1 or 3 blocks, an
indexer equal to its target plus noise plus a direct and a spill tilt from −1
to +2 target-score SDs (42 tilts), and a target that attends strongly or
weakly to literal matches; 1,200 families per scenario, common random numbers.
